## The secrecy myth

Most quant secrecy protects capacity-constrained execution details, not ideas. The ideas themselves — factor construction, regime detection, validation methodology — improve dramatically when exposed to peer review.

The execution edge — low-latency infrastructure, market microstructure insights, capacity constraints — that's worth protecting. But the research methodology? That only gets better in the open.

## Where the line sits

The useful split is between method and execution. Method — factor construction,
validation design, the data you used and the choices you made — costs you nothing to
publish and gets better when other people can attack it. Execution is the part worth
keeping quiet about.

- **Method:** the construction, the data sources, the parameter choices, and the results that did not work
- **Validation:** walk-forward design, out-of-sample splits, regime-aware testing
- **Tooling:** the scripts and pipelines that produced the numbers
- **Execution:** low-latency infrastructure, order routing, capacity — this is the part that stays private

That is the standard we hold writing to here, and the standard we would ask of anything
we published.

## The benefits of openness

1. **Faster iteration:** Peer review catches mistakes you miss
2. **Better methodology:** Collective scrutiny improves validation standards
3. **Community building:** Open research attracts collaborators, not competitors
4. **Reproducibility:** Others can verify your results, building trust
